Validation

Do not take anyone's word for it.

A payment is worth what the rules behind it are worth, and the rules are only real for someone who checks them. A Genoryn Krypton full node checks every block it receives, in full, before it believes anything in it. It trusts no miner, no website and no other node.

Imagine being handed a banknote and being able to test, in two milliseconds, that it was printed by the rules and that there will never be more than 21 million of them. That is what a full node does with every GNR, including the ones nobody has paid you yet.

What is checked

Every rule, on every block

The work

Each block's KryptoHash proof of work, against a difficulty that the node computes itself from the 90 blocks before it. A block with too little work is not a block.

Every proof

Each transfer carries a zk-STARK proof that its inputs exist, belong to the spender and add up to its outputs and fee. The node verifies every one, in about 2 ms each. A transfer that creates GNR from nothing has no valid proof.

No coin spent twice

Spending a note reveals its nullifier. The node keeps every nullifier ever seen and refuses a block that repeats one.

The supply

5 GNR per block, halving every 2,100,000 blocks, 21,000,000 in all. The node adds the reward up itself; a block that pays out more is refused whatever work stands behind it.

Who is paid

Mining rewards go to a pool and are paid by shares. The node checks every share's work and every payout against the accounts it keeps, so no miner can pay itself another's reward.

The chain itself

Timestamps, sizes, the note tree's root, the commitment to all earlier blocks. When two chains compete, the node follows the valid one with the most work, and no other.

The light wallet

Stronger than "simple payment verification", and honest about its limit

Most light wallets of other currencies ask a server and believe the answer. The Genoryn Krypton wallet does not. Every block header commits to all blocks before it and to the work in them, so the wallet can check, with a few hundred samples, that the chain it is shown is the one with the most work; it refuses blocks dated in the future, a chain without the release's checkpoint, and any chain lighter than one it has already seen. Then it checks every block it reads against that block's header.

What it does not do is re-verify the proofs inside other people's transactions. For that it relies on the miners who built on the block, like every light wallet does. A full node relies on nobody.

For everyday amounts the light wallet is a sound choice. If you accept large payments, run a business on GNR, or simply want to depend on no one, run a full node and point your wallet at it.

Who decides

Validation is what keeps the rules yours

No company, foundation or person controls Genoryn Krypton, and no key can change its rules. The rules are whatever the people who accept GNR check for. A miner can produce any block it likes; if the nodes of the people being paid refuse it, it is worth nothing.

That only works while enough people check. If almost everyone used someone else's node, a handful of miners and services could agree to change the rules, the supply limit included, and most users would never notice. Each person who verifies their own payments makes that harder for everyone else as well.

So the most useful thing you can do for Genoryn Krypton, after using it, is to validate the payments you receive with your own full node.

In practice

Do you validate your payments?

Running a node somewhere is not the same as using it. You validate when the wallet you are paid with gets its data from a node you run. There are three ways:

  1. The wallet's own node

    In the wallet open Network, choose Full node and press Save. The wallet starts kryptond, which downloads and checks the whole chain, and from then on asks only it.

  2. A node on another computer of yours

    Run kryptond on a computer that is always on, and enter its address under Network → Nodes for light mode in the wallet on your laptop. Your wallet still verifies what it is told, and now it is told by you.

  3. The command line

    For servers and businesses: kryptond with krypton-wallet-cli --node http://127.0.0.1:30334, or your own program on the node's API.
