Requirements

It runs on the computer you have.

Genoryn Krypton was built so that checking your own money does not take a data centre. The light wallet downloads no chain at all. A full node, which checks every rule since the first block, fits on any disk today. The numbers below were measured, and they say when.

Later

How fast the chain grows

A full node keeps every block. At block 21,800 that is about 0.5 GB: on average about 25 KB a block, or some 35 MB a day at today's use. Blocks have a hard ceiling of about 4 MiB, so if every block were full the chain could grow by up to 5.8 GB a day. That would take about 40 transfers every minute, around the clock; today there are a handful an hour.

The light wallet is not affected by any of this. Its work grows with the logarithm of the chain's length, and it reads only the blocks since the wallet was created.

Not yet: a node cannot discard old blocks (pruning), and it cannot start from a snapshot. Both would shorten the first sync and the disk needed, and neither exists today. A node downloads and checks everything.

Before you start

Things to know

  • Data caps. A full node that accepts incoming connections sends blocks to other nodes that are syncing. If your connection is metered, keep the node outbound-only (the wallet's node is, by default) or limit its peers. See tuning.
  • Antivirus. A miner uses your graphics card at full power, and some antivirus programs do not like the look of that. The release files are signed; verify what you downloaded rather than switching protection off.
  • Heat and power. Mining draws real electricity. The wallet lets you cap processor use; nothing limits a graphics card but the card.
  • The law. Using, mining or running a node for a cryptocurrency is restricted in some countries. Check what applies where you live.
