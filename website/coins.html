What a Krypton coin is

Private like GNR. Honest about its issuer.

Every transfer is hidden

Coins live in the same shielded note tree as GNR. A transfer proves, in zero knowledge, that the coin and the amounts balance; the chain sees only a proof. There is no transparent mode, for any coin.

Quantum-safe

Spends are zk-STARK proofs over hash functions, notes are encrypted with X25519 + ML-KEM-1024 and issuers sign with ML-DSA-87. Nothing that guards a coin depends on elliptic curves.

Declared, not hidden in code

No smart contracts. A coin is a short manifest: its supply, whether the issuer can create more, whether holders can burn, whether the issuer can pause. It is fixed at deploy, part of the coin's id, and shown to every holder in plain words.

GNR is the fuel and the market

Every fee is paid in GNR, deploying a coin costs 10 GNR (to the miners), and coins trade only against GNR, in one atomic transaction. Nobody can sell you a coin they do not hand over.

No impostors of the base layer

Nobody can deploy a coin named after GNR, and the Bitcoin name belongs only to the project's Bitcoin Krypton: everyone else's BTCK is refused by consensus. Tickers and names are unique: the first deploy owns them.

Supply anyone can audit

Transfers are private, but issues and burns are public, so every coin's total supply is known to everyone, at every block, without trusting anyone.

How a swap works

Both sides move, or nothing does.

1

The buyer

prepares its GNR payment to the seller, without the proof that makes it valid.

2

The seller

checks that it receives the agreed GNR, adds the coin for the buyer and proves its half.

3

The buyer

checks that it receives the agreed coin, proves its half and sends the one transaction.

4

Done

The chain sees one private transfer. Unfinished swaps simply expire: nothing is locked.

Risk disclosure

Read the risks of a coin before you accept it.

A Krypton coin is only as good as its issuer. The network guarantees that nobody can create, take or freeze coins in ways the manifest does not declare, but it cannot guarantee that a dollar coin is worth a dollar. Wallets show these labels under every coin, generated from its manifest:

LabelWhat it means
Fixed supplyExactly the cap was issued, once. Nobody, not even the issuer, can ever create more.
The issuer can create more, up to NMore units can appear, but never beyond the declared cap.
The issuer can create any amount at any timeNo cap. Normal for a stable coin backed by reserves (the issuer issues against deposits), dangerous for anything else.
Holders can burn their coinsCoins can be destroyed, publicly; a backed coin is usually redeemed this way.
The issuer can stop all transfersThe issuer may pause the coin for everyone. Transfers of such a coin show which coin moves (never who or how much), so a pause can be enforced.
Its value depends on the issuer holding the reservesIssuer-backed coins (like USDK) are claims on the issuer, who must keep the reserves and honour redemptions. The network cannot check bank accounts.
Custodial bridgeA bridged coin (like a future SOLK) is worth what the custodian holds on the original chain.
Not backed by anythingIts value is only what people pay for it.
Not verifiedAnyone can deploy a coin with any name. Without verification, the name says nothing about who is behind it.
Ticker of a major coinAn unverified coin named like ETHK, SOLK or USDTK may be an impersonation.

What no issuer can ever do, whatever the manifest says: move your coins, see your balance, freeze one holder, change the terms, or issue a coin's units without the issuer's post-quantum key. Coins are not GNR: they cannot pay fees and never count in your GNR balance.

Verification policy

Verified means one thing: we know who issues it.

  • What it is. The project keeps a list of coins whose issuer it identified, signed with the same post-quantum release key (ML-DSA-87 + SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s) that signs every wallet release, under its own context so neither signature can stand in for the other. It is published at /verified-assets.json with its signature at /verified-assets.json.sig, and built into every wallet.
  • What wallets do with it. They trust no list that is not signed. Verified coins get a check mark, their checked issuer name and their logo; logos of other coins are never shown, so nobody can dress a fake in a real coin's colours.
  • What it is not. It is not an endorsement, an audit of reserves or a promise of value. A verified coin can still lose its value if its issuer fails.
  • How to apply. Open an issue on GitHub with the coin id, the issuer's identity and evidence for its backing. Bridges and stable coins need public proof of reserves.
  • Consensus does not depend on it. Any coin can be deployed and moved without verification; the list only labels coins in wallets and on this site.
Stable coins

Dollar, Euro and Bitcoin Krypton.

Dollar Krypton · USDK

A private, quantum-safe dollar. Issued by its issuer against US dollar reserves the issuer holds, redeemed by burning. Mintable without a cap (it issues against deposits), burnable by holders, and it can never be paused.

Euro Krypton · EURK

The same for the euro: issuer-backed, mintable against deposits, burnable, never pausable. Its transfers are as private as GNR's.

Bitcoin Krypton · BTCK

Bitcoin's price, on a private, quantum-safe chain: one BTCK for each bitcoin the issuer holds, so it follows the price of bitcoin. At most 21,000,000, redeemed by burning, never pausable. Only the project's issuer key can deploy it.

All three are issuer-backed coins: their value depends on the issuer actually holding the reserves and honouring redemptions. They appear in the list above with their manifests and live supply once deployed (USDK and EURK from block 17,100, BTCK from block 18,600). Manifests: usdk.kam · eurk.kam · btck.kam.

Reserves

USDK, EURK and BTCK are issued only against reserves the issuer holds: one US dollar per USDK, one euro per EURK, one bitcoin per BTCK. Their supply on chain is public at every block (issued minus burned, in the list above). The issuer publishes here what it holds against that supply. Until the first issue the supply is zero and there is nothing to back.

Issue and trade

Issue units (public), send them privately, and list them on the coin market against GNR.

Or from the command line:

krypton-wallet-cli coin new --name "Dollar Krypton" --ticker USDK --decimals 2 \
    --supply mintable --burn --backing issuer-reserves --reserves https://example.org/reserves --out usdk.kam
krypton-wallet-cli coin deploy usdk.kam
krypton-wallet-cli coin issue USDK <address> 1000
krypton-wallet-cli coin send USDK <address> 25.50
krypton-wallet-cli coin info USDK

Bridged coins. A bridge can bring another chain's coin here as a custodial coin (for example SOLK for SOL, origin solana:SOL), declared with backing: bridge. The custodian holds the originals; its coins carry the custodial risk label.

Trading

One transaction. Nothing to trust.

Coins trade against GNR on the coin market in the wallet. A swap is a single transaction: the seller's coin spend and the buyer's GNR spend are bound to the same signed hash, so either both happen or neither does. Each wallet checks what it will receive before it adds its own proof, and on chain the swap looks like any other private transfer.

A coin's market price is the plain average of its completed swaps in the last 72 hours. The board counts a swap only after checking it against the mined transaction (the swap's two crossing outputs are opened with one-time keys), so a report cannot invent a price, and offers never move it.

FAQ

Questions about Krypton coins.

What is a Krypton coin?

A coin issued by anyone on Genoryn Krypton. It is held and sent like GNR, always shielded and quantum-safe, and its issuer's powers are declared in a public manifest that can never change.

Are coin transfers really private?

Yes. The coin, the amount, the sender and the recipient are hidden by a zk-STARK proof, exactly like GNR. Only issues and burns are public, so supplies can be audited. Coins whose issuer may pause them show which coin moves, never who or how much.

Can an issuer freeze or take my coins?

No issuer can take coins, see balances or freeze a single holder. If, and only if, the manifest says so, the issuer can pause all transfers of that coin at once; every wallet shows that risk before you accept it.

Can someone make a fake USDK?

A ticker belongs to the first coin that deploys it, and the names of GNR and Bitcoin are refused outright. Anyone can still choose a similar name, which is why wallets show the verified mark, the checked issuer and the logo only for coins in the signed verified list.

What does it cost?

Deploying a coin costs 10 GNR, paid to miners. Every transfer pays an ordinary network fee in GNR (about twice a GNR transfer's, since it carries two proofs).

What is Bitcoin Krypton (BTCK)?

A bitcoin-backed coin: the issuer holds one bitcoin for each BTCK, so BTCK follows the price of bitcoin while moving privately and quantum-safely on Genoryn Krypton. Like USDK it is a claim on the issuer's reserves, redeemed by burning.

Is USDK worth a dollar?

USDK is an issuer-backed coin: it is worth a dollar only if its issuer holds the reserves and redeems it. The network guarantees the supply and the privacy, not the reserves.
