Why swap bitcoin for GNR

Move bitcoin's value into a quantum-resistant system.

Bitcoin's coins are guarded by elliptic-curve signatures (ECDSA and Schnorr). A large enough quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could break them and spend other people's coins. Nobody knows when such a machine will exist, but savings are kept for decades.

That is what this market is for. An atomic swap lets you move value from bitcoin into GNR, whose coins are guarded by hash-based zk-STARK proofs instead of elliptic curves, directly from your own wallet: no exchange holds your coins, no account, no identity. You can swap back to bitcoin the same way whenever you like.

  • For protectionValue you move into GNR no longer depends on elliptic-curve cryptography.
  • ProvenA sell offer is listed only after the board has checked that its GNR is there.
  • AtomicEither both sides are paid, or both keep their coins. Nobody can run off with half a trade.
  • Locked firstThe seller's GNR is locked on chain before the buyer sends any bitcoin.
  • Honest about limitsA swap exchanges bitcoin for GNR at the market price; the bitcoin itself stays on Bitcoin's chain with its new owner.

Rules

What an offer commits you to

The same for everyone. The wallet shows them before you publish.

Selling GNR

  1. You can only offer GNR you have. Publishing sets the GNR aside in a note of its own, and the board lists the offer only after it has checked a zero-knowledge proof that it is there.
  2. It stays set aside while the offer is open. If it moves, the board takes the offer down at once, and the same GNR cannot back two offers.
  3. Publishing commits the GNR for 7 days. Within that time it can be sold, or put into another sell offer, but not sent. If you cancel, or the offer runs its time unsold, the wallet lets it be sent again 7 days after the day you published. Offer only what you mean to sell.
  4. You choose who can take it. By default only a buyer whose bitcoin is already deposited: your wallet sees the bitcoin on the Bitcoin chain before it locks anything, and the buyer's wallet pays by itself. Or any buyer, paying by hand; then your wallet locks for at most two unpaid buyers at a time.
  5. When a buyer takes the offer, the wallet locks exactly that GNR for them on chain. If they never pay, it returns to you when the lock ends, about 21 hours later.

Buying GNR

  1. A buy offer is paid in advance, to yourself. Publishing shows an address of your own wallet. Once your bitcoin is there and confirmed, the board checks it on the Bitcoin chain and lists the offer with a ✓.
  2. When a seller takes it, you do nothing. Their GNR is locked for you on chain; once that lock has confirmed, your wallet pays the swap contract from your deposit and then claims the GNR.
  3. Your bitcoin goes into a contract, never to a person, and only after the GNR is locked for you: the seller can take it only by revealing the secret that releases the GNR to you. If the seller never takes it, it comes back when the contract's time lock ends.
  4. If you cancel, or nobody sells, the wallet sends the bitcoin back to your address. It cannot wait in the swap contract itself, because that contract names the seller's key and there is no seller yet.
  5. Taking a sell offer works the same way: you pay once, to an address of your wallet, and the swap starts and completes by itself; if the offer is gone meanwhile, the bitcoin comes back. Or pay by hand: the wallet shows where to pay after the seller's GNR is locked for you, and you have two hours.

How a swap works

  1. Get the wallet from the download page and keep it running.
  2. Open Swap: take an offer from the board, or publish your own.
  3. The seller's GNR is locked first. As soon as you take an offer, the seller's wallet locks the GNR on chain, payable only to you. Your wallet checks that lock (exactly the agreed amount, only you can claim it) and shows where to pay only after it has confirmed. A seller who does not have the GNR cannot lock it, so you never pay them.
  4. The wallets finish alone. The seller takes your bitcoin with its secret; your wallet reads that secret from the bitcoin transaction and claims the GNR. If anyone stops, timeouts refund both: the bitcoin after about 3 hours, the GNR after about 21. No swap lasts longer than a week.

How the market price is computed: from what people actually paid, weighed by the bitcoin that moved.

  • Only completed swaps count, and only once their claim is on the GNR chain. Offers and swaps in progress are shown but never move the price: anyone can post an offer at any price.
  • The bitcoin must be on the Bitcoin chain. A swap's report names its bitcoin contract and is signed by one of the two wallets of the swap. The board looks the contract up and notes how much bitcoin was taken out of it with the swap's secret. Nobody else can report a swap, and a report without its bitcoin weighs nothing once there are three that have it.
  • The price is where half of that bitcoin changed hands below, and half above (the volume-weighted median of the last 72 hours, newer swaps weighing more: a swap of 72 hours ago half as much as a new one). A swap never weighs more than its GNR is worth at the going price, and never more than a fifth of the total, so one or two large swaps cannot set the price.
  • Only swaps of at least 0.001 BTC count (since 1.0.48): many tiny swaps between one's own wallets cannot carry the price, and each swap pays two Bitcoin network fees.
  • The price moves at most 10 % an hour. It is rebuilt hour by hour from the swaps themselves, so anyone with the same swaps gets the same price. A real move shows within hours; a burst of swaps cannot jump it. When the swaps are more than 15 % from the price, the board says the price is catching up and Quick Swap waits.
  • Typos are left out: a swap more than 4× away from the median does not count. Until three swaps of at least 0.001 BTC have their bitcoin found, the price is still forming: the plain average of the reported swaps, shown as such.
  • Quick Swap stays near this price: it never takes an offer more than 20 % from the market price (10 % while the price is forming or the market is thin). An offer further away stays on the board, marked far from the market, and can still be taken by hand.

Your price is free: publish an offer at any price you like. The board only refuses offers for more than 1,000 BTC and offers without proof of work; offers whose maker is offline stay listed, greyed out, and cannot be taken. Prices are shown in bitcoin and in dollars at the current BTC price.
