Rules

What an offer commits you to

The same for everyone. The wallet shows them before you publish.

Buying GNR

  1. A buy offer is paid from your Bitcoin balance, to yourself. Publishing moves the offer's bitcoin from your balance to an address of your own wallet made for the offer. Once it is confirmed, the board finds it on the Bitcoin chain and lists the offer with a ✓. A buy offer without bitcoin is not listed at all, and one whose bitcoin leaves is taken down.
  2. When a seller takes it, you do nothing. Their GNR is locked for you on chain; once that lock has confirmed, your wallet pays the swap contract from your deposit and then claims the GNR.
  3. Your bitcoin goes into a contract, never to a person, and only after the GNR is locked for you: the seller can take it only by revealing the secret that releases the GNR to you. If the seller never takes it, it comes back when the contract's time lock ends.
  4. If you cancel, or nobody sells, the wallet sends the bitcoin back to your address. It cannot wait in the swap contract itself, because that contract names the seller's key and there is no seller yet.
  5. Taking a sell offer is paid from your Bitcoin balance too, and from nowhere else: add bitcoin to the balance once, and every purchase starts and completes by itself. If your balance is short, the wallet says by how much. There is no paying by hand: the seller's wallet locks only for bitcoin it can see.

Selling GNR

  1. You can only offer GNR you have. Publishing sets the GNR aside in a note of its own, and the board lists the offer only after it has checked a zero-knowledge proof that it is there.
  2. It stays set aside while the offer is open. If it moves, the board takes the offer down at once, and the same GNR cannot back two offers.
  3. Publishing commits the GNR for 7 days. Within that time it can be sold, or put into another sell offer, but not sent. If you cancel, or the offer runs its time unsold, the wallet lets it be sent again 7 days after the day you published. Offer only what you mean to sell.
  4. Only a buyer with bitcoin can take it. Your wallet sees the buyer's bitcoin on the Bitcoin chain, in the buyer's own wallet, before it locks anything, and the board counts what that bitcoin is promised to. The buyer's wallet then pays by itself. Nobody can take your offer on a promise to pay later.
  5. When a buyer takes the offer, the wallet locks exactly that GNR for them on chain. If they never pay, it returns to you when the lock ends, about 21 hours later.
Price

How the market price is computed

From what people actually paid, weighed by the bitcoin that moved.

  • Only completed swaps count, and only once their claim is on the GNR chain. Offers and swaps in progress are shown but never move the price: anyone can post an offer at any price.
  • The bitcoin must be on the Bitcoin chain. A swap's report names its bitcoin contract and is signed by one of the two wallets of the swap. The board looks the contract up and notes how much bitcoin was taken out of it with the swap's secret. Nobody else can report a swap, and a report without its bitcoin weighs nothing once there are three that have it.
  • The price is where half of that bitcoin changed hands below, and half above (a weighted median, method gnr-price-2 since 1.0.53). It uses the shortest window with enough swaps: the last 24 hours when the market is busy, otherwise 72 hours, 7 days or 30 days, newer swaps weighing more (one at the window's end half as much as a new one). A swap never weighs more than its GNR is worth at the going price, never more than a fifth of the total, and one 6-hour block never more than a quarter, so one or two large swaps, or a burst in one afternoon, cannot set the price.
  • Only swaps of at least 0.001 BTC count (since 1.0.48): many tiny swaps between one's own wallets cannot carry the price, and each swap pays two Bitcoin network fees.
  • The price moves at most 10 % an hour. It is rebuilt hour by hour from the swaps themselves, so anyone with the same swaps gets the same price. A real move shows within hours; a burst of swaps cannot jump it. When the swaps are more than 15 % from the price, the board says the price is catching up and Quick Swap waits.
  • Typos are left out: a swap more than 4× away from the median does not count, nor one further from it than five times the typical deviation (a swap within 50 % is always kept). Until three swaps of at least 0.001 BTC have their bitcoin found, the price is still forming: the plain average of the reported swaps, shown as such.
  • Every price says how far to trust it: a confidence from 0 to 100 from the bitcoin behind it, how many swaps effectively count, how closely they agree and how fresh the newest is, and a status (reliable, moderate, low liquidity, low confidence, stale, insufficient). Anyone can recompute the price: the wallet console's verifymarketprice does it from the trade history.
  • Quick Swap stays near this price: it never takes an offer more than 20 % from the market price (10 % while the price is forming or the market is thin). An offer further away stays on the board, marked far from the market, and can still be taken there one at a time.

Your price is free: publish an offer at any price you like. The board only refuses offers for more than 1,000 BTC and offers without proof of work; offers whose maker is offline stay listed, greyed out, and cannot be taken. Prices are shown in bitcoin and in dollars at the current BTC price.
