GNR market

People set the price of GNR themselves: with the offers they publish in the wallet and the swaps they complete. Every trade is an atomic swap between two wallets, so there is no exchange, no custody and no account.

How to trade

  1. Get the wallet from the download page and keep it running.
  2. Open Swap: take an offer from the board, or publish your own at any price.
  3. The wallets do the rest. The buyer pays bitcoin into a contract, and the seller's GNR is locked only after that deposit confirms. Either both get paid, or both get their coins back.

How the market price is computed: a robust, volume-weighted median of everything people are doing on the board.

  • Swaps completed count three times per GNR. A swap only counts if its claim is on chain.
  • Swaps in progress count twice.
  • Open buy and sell offers count once, if their maker's wallet is online, so the first offer already sets a price.
  • Older data fades: half its weight every 12 hours, gone after 72.
  • Absurd prices are left out: anything more than 4× away from the typical price, so nobody can drag it with one joke offer.

The GNR price is that market price, with the cost to mine one GNR as its floor. Above the cost, the market alone sets the price. Below it, price = depth × market + (1 − depth) × cost to mine: miners rarely sell below what a coin costs to make, so only a deep market can push GNR under it. Depth grows with the GNR traded behind the price (half at 25 GNR, nearly all at a few hundred). The cost rises as more miners join, because the same 7,200 GNR a day then take more electricity, and the floor rises with it.

Cost to mine assumes RTX 5090-class GPUs at $0.15 per kWh. Prices are shown in sat and in dollars at the current BTC price.

Swaps on mainnet activate at block 3,300.
