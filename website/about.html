What is Genoryn Krypton?

Genoryn Krypton (GNR) is a quantum-resistant, private-by-default proof-of-work cryptocurrency. It keeps Bitcoin's monetary design (21,000,000 coins, halvings about every four years, mining, no premine) and rebuilds the cryptography for the quantum era: coins are protected by hash-based zk-STARK proofs instead of elliptic curves, every transfer is shielded, and payment data is encrypted with NIST's post-quantum ML-KEM.

Genoryn Krypton and Bitcoin

Genoryn Krypton follows Bitcoin's monetary rules almost exactly: a fixed cap of 21,000,000 coins, a block reward that halves about every four years, open proof-of-work mining and a fair launch with no premine. Its launch is even tied to Bitcoin: the genesis block commits to the hash of Bitcoin block 974,438, so no GNR could be mined before that moment, not even by its author.

What it adds is what Bitcoin does not have:

  • Quantum resistance. Bitcoin's coins are protected by elliptic-curve signatures (ECDSA and Schnorr on secp256k1), which a large quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could break. GNR is protected by hash functions only.
  • Privacy by default. Bitcoin transactions show every amount and address forever. GNR transfers hide them for everyone, always.
  • Fair mining without pools. Every block's reward is split among all miners by their work, so a single computer earns its share every block.
  • Wallets that verify. Light wallets check the chain themselves with proofs of work and Merkle Mountain Range proofs, without trusting a server.

In short: Bitcoin's monetary design, rebuilt for the quantum era and private by default. Genoryn Krypton is an independent network with its own code and chain; it is not a fork of Bitcoin and is not affiliated with Bitcoin's developers. The two are connected in practice by atomic swaps: GNR and bitcoin are exchanged directly between wallets.

Why quantum resistance matters

Almost every cryptocurrency, Bitcoin included, rests on elliptic-curve cryptography. A sufficiently large quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could derive private keys from public keys and spend other people's coins, and could decrypt private transactions recorded today ("harvest now, decrypt later"). Money meant to hold value for decades cannot depend on that assumption.

Genoryn Krypton's protection of funds and privacy uses no elliptic curve at all:

  • Spending is a zero-knowledge proof (zk-STARK) of knowing a secret behind a hash. STARKs need no trusted setup and rely only on collision-resistant hashes, which quantum computers weaken only quadratically (Grover's algorithm); 256-bit hashes keep a 128-bit margin.
  • Encryption of payment data is hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-1024 (NIST FIPS 203), secure as long as either part holds.
  • Releases are signed with ML-DSA-87 and SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s (NIST FIPS 204 and 205), both of which must verify.
  • Every algorithm has a versioned identifier, so a weakened one can be replaced by a soft fork.

How it works, in five lines

  1. Notes: coins are private notes; spending reveals only a nullifier that prevents double spending.
  2. Proofs: each transfer carries a ~92 KB zk-STARK proof, built in about half a second and verified in about 2 ms.
  3. Mining: KryptoHash is memory-bandwidth bound; about 32 shares per block share every reward by work.
  4. Wallets: light wallets verify sampled headers, total work and every block they read; keys never leave the device.
  5. Swaps: GNR and bitcoin are exchanged with hash and time locks; the seller's GNR is locked before the buyer pays.

Frequently asked questions

What is Genoryn Krypton?

A quantum-resistant, private-by-default proof-of-work cryptocurrency (ticker GNR) with Bitcoin's monetary design: 21 million coins, halvings, mining and a fair launch with no premine.

Is Genoryn Krypton a fork of Bitcoin?

No. It is an independent network with its own code and chain. It follows Bitcoin's monetary rules, its launch is tied to a Bitcoin block hash, and it trades with bitcoin through atomic swaps, but it shares no code or coins with Bitcoin.

What makes it quantum-resistant?

Funds are protected by zk-STARK proofs over hash functions, not by elliptic curves, so Shor's algorithm does not apply. Payment data is encrypted with hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-1024, and releases are signed with ML-DSA and SLH-DSA.

Is it anonymous?

Transfers hide amounts, senders, recipients and memos for everyone, always. Mining payouts are public per miner, as on every chain; mine to a separate wallet to keep them unlinked.

How is it different from Monero and Zcash?

Monero and Zcash protect funds with elliptic-curve cryptography; GNR uses hash-based zk-STARKs and post-quantum encryption. Unlike Zcash, every GNR transfer is shielded; unlike Monero, supply is capped at 21 million.

How can I get GNR?

Mine it with a GPU or CPU from the wallet, or buy it with bitcoin through an atomic swap on the offer board. There was no presale and GNR is not listed on exchanges.

Who created it, and who controls it?

It was created by the pseudonymous Zarazi Sokani. Nobody controls the protocol: rules change only when node operators choose to run new software.

Has it been audited?

It has had an internal audit and a privately reported external review, whose findings are fixed. A full independent audit has not been done yet.
