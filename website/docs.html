Overview

Genoryn Krypton (ticker GNR) is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency built so that no elliptic curve protects your coins or your privacy. Spends are authorized by zk-STARK proofs over hash functions, every transfer is shielded, and notes are encrypted with NIST's post-quantum ML-KEM. Light wallets verify the chain themselves without downloading it.

Quick start

  1. Install. Download the Windows installer or the Linux package from Download, or build from source with cargo build --release.
  2. Create a wallet. Open krypton-wallet, choose a password and write down the recovery phrase (24 words, each with a two-digit number) on paper.
  3. Get coins. Mine from the wallet's Mining tab, or share your short address (gnrs1…) to receive.
  4. Send privately. Enter an address and amount; a zero-knowledge proof is built on your device in about half a second.
krypton-wallet-cli new                               # create a wallet
krypton-wallet-cli --node https://genoryn.io balance  # verified light sync
krypton-wallet-cli --node https://genoryn.io send gnrs1... 2.5 --memo "invoice 42"

The wallet

The desktop wallet runs a local interface on 127.0.0.1 protected by a random session token. Keys, scanning and proving never leave your computer.

  • Light mode (default): remote nodes provide data; headers, outputs and tree updates are verified locally.
  • Full node mode: one click starts kryptond; the wallet then uses your own node.
  • Mining: choose Automatic, NVIDIA (CUDA), AMD/Intel (OpenCL) or CPU, with a CPU-usage cap.
  • Short addresses: full addresses are ~2,600 characters because they carry a post-quantum key. Press Publish short form once (it goes to genoryn.io, your configured nodes and your own node, and the wallet shows where it resolves), then share the short form; nodes resolve it and the wallet checks the hash.
  • Overview: under the balance, live tiles and charts for network hashrate, estimated miners, block time, shares, difficulty, GNR issued, your mining share and rewards, plus the latest blocks with the ones you found marked.
  • Background mining: while the miner runs, closing the window keeps the wallet, node and miner running; the desktop icon opens it again and Quit stops everything.
  • Recovery: 24 words with their numbers (384-bit entropy, checksummed) restore everything, and older 24-word phrases restore too; the wallet file is encrypted with Argon2id (256 MiB) and XChaCha20-Poly1305.
  • Several wallets: the Wallets tab holds any number of wallets, each with its own 24 words and password, and shows their total. An unlocked wallet stays open for 24 hours, then locks itself and asks for its password again; the miner keeps running. Removing a wallet moves its file to a removed folder, and its 24 words bring it back.

A transaction spends at most two notes. When a payment needs more, the wallet merges notes automatically in parallel rounds (one block per round) and then pays.

Privacy model

Coins live as notes: (owner, value, randomness) hidden inside a commitment cm in a Merkle tree of depth 40. Spending a note reveals only its nullifier, a hash of a secret key and the note position, which prevents double spending without revealing which note was spent.

Mining income is public per miner (as coinbase rewards are on every chain); mine to a separate wallet if you want it unlinked from your receiving address. Spending mined coins is as private as any other spend. There is no transparent mode, so every user shares the same anonymity set. Peer connections are encrypted end to end; your own transactions are first relayed to a single random peer and only announced to everyone after 20 seconds, which hides their origin.

Keys derive deterministically from the recovery phrase: 24 tokens word-NN (an English BIP-39 word and a number 00–63, 17 bits each) carry 384 bits of entropy and a 24-bit SHA3 checksum, a 192-bit margin even against Grover's quantum search. SHAKE256 turns them into a spend secret, a hybrid KEM key pair and an ML-DSA key. The spend secret yields an authorization key ak, a nullifier key nk and an owner tag H(ak, nk); view-only wallets can hold nk without being able to spend.

Quantum security

Shor's algorithm breaks RSA and elliptic curves (ECDSA, Ed25519, Pallas, BLS). None of them protect Genoryn Krypton coins: spends are proofs of knowledge of a hash preimage, verified with hash-based STARKs. Encryption is hybrid, so recorded traffic stays secret as long as either X25519 or ML-KEM holds ("harvest now, decrypt later" is defeated by ML-KEM).

Grover's algorithm square-roots brute force: 256-bit hashes and keys keep a 128-bit margin; STARK soundness parameters target about 160 conjectured bits.

Every primitive has a versioned identifier, so a weakened algorithm can be replaced by a soft fork without stopping the network. No independent audit has been completed yet; see Security model.

Emission & supply

The schedule follows Bitcoin's shape on 60-second blocks. Block h ≥ 1 issues 5 GNR >> floor((h − 1) / 2,100,000) into the reward pool; the genesis issues nothing. Fees join the pool too, and the pool pays miners in proportion to their shares (Mining rewards).

Fair launch

The mainnet genesis commits to the hash of a Bitcoin block mined after the launch is announced. That hash is unpredictable, so no Genoryn Krypton block can exist before the announced moment, and everyone, the author included, starts together. Mainnet commits to Bitcoin block 974,438 (00000000000000000001075275c9ea6c90ce99dd94214d855d2b726fb173bed9), the newest Bitcoin block when the network launched; genesis hash 3c10136cd72a74d673257249752522e0102b6268f5b8c348ba34ae36740ca3b3.

  1. Announce a Bitcoin height H at least 72 hours ahead.
  2. Wait for block H; publish the release whose genesis contains its hash.
  3. Anyone rebuilds from source and compares the genesis hash.
  4. Mining opens for everyone at the same time under the same rules.

Mining rewards

Genoryn Krypton splits every block's reward among all miners by the work they did, without pools and without luck deciding who takes a whole block.

  1. Shares. A header whose proof of work meets difficulty / 32 is a share. The network finds about 32 per block; the block itself is one of its miner's shares. Shares are relayed peer to peer and credited by any block within the next 32.
  2. Pool. Each block adds subsidy + fees to a consensus-held pool. Every credited share earns pool · weight / (8 · 32 · difficulty): the same slice for everyone, so 1 % of the shares earns 1 % of the rewards.
  3. No incentive to censor. A share's credit never depends on which other shares a block includes, so leaving out other miners' shares gains a block finder nothing, and those shares are credited by the next block.
  4. Payouts. Credits accrue per miner and are paid as one note every 360 blocks (~6 hours), at most 64 payouts per block, spendable after 100 blocks.

KryptoHash algorithm

Memory-hard and GPU-first: each hash reads 64 random 128-byte pairs from a dataset, so throughput follows memory bandwidth.

  • Epoch: 16,384 blocks (~11.4 days); seed from the genesis hash and the epoch number.
  • Cache: 16 MiB, BLAKE3 chain plus three RandMemoHash passes.
  • Dataset: 2 GiB at launch, +4 MiB per epoch, capped at 12 GiB; each 64-byte item mixes 64 cache items.
  • Hash: 128-byte mix, 64 dataset accesses with FNV and rotations, final BLAKE3.
  • Target: hash · difficulty < 2^256, difficulty is a 128-bit integer.
  • Verification: needs only the cache: about 1 ms per block, so light clients can check proofs of work.

Consensus rules

A 332-byte header commits to the previous block, a Merkle Mountain Range of all earlier blocks, the transactions, the shares, the note-tree root, the nullifiers, the outputs, the payouts and its miner. A block is valid when:

  1. It extends its parent; its timestamp exceeds the median of the last 11 and is at most 300 s in the future.
  2. Its difficulty equals the LWMA value and its proof of work meets it.
  3. Its MMR, transaction and share roots, output and nullifier digests match.
  4. It is at most 4 MiB with at most 256 shares, each valid for its window and never credited before.
  5. Every proof verifies, nullifiers are unique, anchors are recent (≤ 2,880 blocks).
  6. Its payouts equal those computed by the reward rules.
  7. The note tree after appending matured payout notes and transfer outputs has the committed root.

Fork choice: the valid chain with the most cumulative work. Side branches are checked for proof of work before storage and fully validated when they become heavier. Full specification: docs/spec/consensus.md.

Payment proofs

Transfers are private, yet the sender can prove a payment. When you send, the wallet keeps the one-time key of the payment output. Proof in the History tab (or krypton-wallet-cli prove-payment <txid>) turns it into a short gnrpay1… text.

  1. The verifier's wallet (Verify a payment proof, or verify-payment) finds the transaction's block and checks it against the chain.
  2. It decrypts exactly that output with the disclosed key and checks that it commits to the recipient's owner tag.
  3. It shows amount, memo, recipient, block and confirmations, and whether the payment went to the verifying wallet itself.

The proof reveals nothing else: not the sender's balance, other notes, change or keys. Only the sender (who made the key) and the recipient can produce one. A 128-bit checksum rejects incomplete or altered proofs.

Transactions

Every transfer spends two notes (one may be a zero-value dummy) and creates two outputs (payment and change). The STARK proof shows that the inputs exist under a recent tree root, that their nullifiers are correct, that the outputs commit to the stated owners and values, that all values are below 2^60 and that inputs equal outputs plus fee.

Light clients

Servers are trusted for availability only:

  1. Tip: sampled headers (dense near the tip, sparse across history) are checked for proof of work and MMR inclusion; the recent 100 headers are checked fully. Cost grows with log(chain length).
  2. Blocks: compact blocks since the wallet birthday are checked against their headers: outputs, nullifiers and the note-tree root.
  3. Witnesses: the wallet downloads a whole 4,096-leaf chunk and computes its own Merkle path.
  4. Cross-checks: several configured nodes must agree; transactions are sent through all of them.

Exchange integration

  1. Run your own kryptond; never rely on third-party nodes for deposits.
  2. Use a dedicated wallet; distinguish customers by memo or by account.
  3. Credit after 10 confirmations (more for large amounts).
  4. Withdraw with krypton-wallet-cli send; consolidation is automatic.
  5. Validate user-entered addresses with /v1/validate or krypton_address_network.

Security model

  • Status: extensive automated tests (consensus, proofs, reorganizations, forged light-client data, parser property tests), no independent audit yet.
  • Chain observer: sees fees, sizes, timing; not amounts, senders or recipients.
  • Malicious server: may withhold data, cannot forge it undetected; use several sources.
  • Quantum adversary: no elliptic-curve dependency for funds or privacy.
  • Your computer: a compromised machine is out of scope; keep the recovery phrase offline.

Report vulnerabilities privately via GitHub security advisories; see SECURITY.md.

Upgrades & 100-year design

  • Crypto-agility: every primitive has a versioned ID; replacements activate by soft fork.
  • Room to grow: a depth-40 note tree holds about one trillion outputs.
  • Log-time verification: the header MMR keeps light clients fast at any chain length.
  • Bounded resources: fixed headers, 4 MiB blocks, bounded decoders and mempool.
  • Roadmap: recursive proof aggregation per block, hash-time-locked notes for atomic swaps, external audits.

Governance

Genoryn Krypton was created by the pseudonymous Zarazi Sokani, whose identity is unknown and will never be revealed. Anyone may develop, market or extend the project in any direction. No person, company or foundation controls the protocol or holds keys over it; rules change only when node operators choose to run new software.

FAQ

Do I need a GPU?

No. A GPU is about 50 times faster, but CPUs mine too, and the wallet works without mining at all.

Why are addresses so long?

They carry a post-quantum public key (ML-KEM-1024). Use the short form for sharing; the wallet verifies it.

Can anyone see my balance?

No. Balances are computed only by wallets that hold the keys.

What happens if a node lies to my light wallet?

Its data fails verification against headers and proof of work, and the wallet refuses it.

Is there a premine?

No. The genesis issues nothing and the launch is bound to a future Bitcoin block hash.

Glossary

Note
A private coin: owner, value and randomness hidden in a commitment.
Nullifier
A unique tag published when a note is spent; prevents double spends.
Anchor
A recent note-tree root that a spend proves membership against.
zk-STARK
A hash-based zero-knowledge proof; no trusted setup, quantum-resistant.
ML-KEM
NIST's post-quantum key encapsulation (FIPS 203).
MMR
Merkle Mountain Range: an append-only commitment to all block hashes.
LWMA
Linearly weighted moving average difficulty algorithm.
gori
The smallest unit: 1 GNR = 100,000,000 gori.
